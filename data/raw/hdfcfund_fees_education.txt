What Is Expense Ratio in Mutual Funds?
Last Updated On: 15 May 2026 | Created On: 3 Jul 2025
5 min read
Expense ratio is a crucial factor to consider when investing in mutual fund schemes. It represents the annual fee charged by a fund house for managing an investor’s money. The expense ratio is expressed as a percentage of the fund’s total assets under management (AUM) and directly impacts the investor’s returns.
Understanding Expense Ratio
The expense ratio includes but not limited to various operational costs such as:
- Fund Management Fees: Payment to the fund manager and investment team.
- Administrative Expenses: Costs related to office operations, record-keeping, and compliance.
- Marketing & Distribution Costs: Fees for advertising, brokerage, and distribution of funds.
- Other Expenses: Custodian fees, legal fees, and audit costs.
How Is Expense Ratio Calculated?
Expense Ratio = (Total Fund Expenses / Total Assets Under Management) × 100
Impact of Expense Ratio on Returns
A high expense ratio reduces net returns over time. For example:
- If a mutual fund possibly generates 12% annual returns but has a 2% expense ratio, the actual return received by the investor will be 10%.
- Over the long term, even a 0.5% difference in expense ratio can significantly impact returns due to compounding effects.

How to Check a Fund’s Expense Ratio?
Investors can find the expense ratio of a mutual fund in:
- The fund’s fact sheet
- SEBI-mandated disclosures on the fund house’s website
Factors to Consider Before Choosing a Fund Based on Expense Ratio
- Investment Objective: A higher expense ratio in an actively managed fund may be justified if it consistently outperforms benchmarks.
- Fund Performance: Compare historical returns net of expenses with peer funds.
- Investment Horizon: For long-term investments, a lower expense ratio helps in compounding gains.
- Fund Category: Actively managed funds tend to have higher expense ratios than passive funds.
Expense ratio plays a vital role in determining net returns. While lower costs are beneficial, investors should also evaluate scheme performance, risk factors, and investment goals before making a decision.
